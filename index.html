Zero Day: Aftermath

VRStealthUnreal Engine 5C++OpenXR

Programmer · Negentropy Studio · final-year project · team of 3

A VR stealth game. You play a broken maintenance droid looking for the last human survivors on a ruined Earth, hunted by the machines that wiped them out. I wrote the gameplay codebase: squad enemy AI with sight and hearing occlusion, corpse detection and flanking.

2026 First-person crawler In development 1.44 MB cap

Floppy Dungeon working title

C++17Win32 · GDINo engineASCII raycasterProcedural

Solo · contest entry · in progress

A first-person dungeon crawler for a contest with one rule: the whole game has to fit in 1,474,560 bytes, the size of a floppy disk, and run on a clean machine with nothing installed.

So there is no engine, no art files and no libraries. Just Win32 and GDI, an ASCII raycaster for the rendering, and lighting, dungeons, biomes and audio all generated at runtime.

How I Build

what stays the same across genres
◇

Systems over scripts

One-off scripted moments age badly. I'd rather build the spawner, the AI senses, the generator: systems that keep surprising you after a hundred runs.

◈

Genre is a brief, not an identity

VR stealth, a cel-shaded FPS, a two-player puzzle game, a dungeon crawler under a byte budget. Different rules every time. The job is working out what this one needs and building that.

▲

Frame rate is a feature

Especially in VR, where dropped frames are felt in the stomach. Custom LOD, hibernation and profiling passes are part of the gameplay work, not an afterthought.

✦

Feel is testable

Reload by hand, drink your health, block with a real shield. If a mechanic can live in the player's body instead of a menu, it should.

●

Small teams, whole ownership

Most of these were made by three people. I've owned entire gameplay codebases end to end: architecture, implementation, debugging, shipping.
